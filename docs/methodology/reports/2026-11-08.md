## 1. Project and claim

MatchPulse is a football tournament platform for local competitions. Spectators
buy tickets, register for a place, follow live match data and trade prediction
points on match outcomes. Points are redeemable for ticket discounts, and
aggregated prediction data is sold to clubs.

The bounded first increment is **capacity-safe tournament registration plus the
live prediction market**. The claim is:

> For a tournament organizer running a local competition, the platform will keep
> participation at or below the configured capacity and keep point balances,
> positions and trades consistent when many spectators act at the same moment,
> measured by a concurrent burst and committed-state assertions against a real
> PostgreSQL database, with the limitation that the evidence is local/CI, uses a
> single database instance, and does not yet include real-user observation.

The claim is deliberately narrow. It does not claim that the whole product vision
is implemented, that the system is production-ready, or that the crowd data has
been validated with clubs.

## 3. Operating model and lifecycle

The project uses a **hybrid lifecycle**:

- **Plan-driven domain contract:** the invariants, authorization rules and release
  policy are fixed constraints. They are not renegotiated per sprint.
- **Iterative and incremental delivery:** small end-to-end increments (capacity,
  market, authorization, idempotency, delivery) are built, tested and released.
- **Adaptive scope:** evidence can change the next increment; the checkpoint
  records and findings register feed the backlog.

This fits the dominant uncertainty: the product direction is bounded, but the
implementation and evidence are discovered incrementally. The lifecycle decision
is recorded in
[`checkpoint-B/04-sdlc-decision.md`](../checkpoint-B/04-sdlc-decision.md).

Work flows through GitHub issues, a short-lived branch, a pull request, CI and a
review decision. The project board uses four stages: Enter, Build, Review Queue,
Release & Learn. The flow evidence is derived from the repository history in
[`checkpoint-B/03-flow-evidence.md`](../checkpoint-B/03-flow-evidence.md); it is
one developer's history, not a team benchmark.

## 6. Checkpoint C — quality and residual risk

Checkpoint C answers the four questions:

1. **Which risk matters most, and why?** The ranking changed over time. Initially
   it was unauthorized access/resolution (R-01/FND-001); after authentication and
   RLS, the top verification risk became the un-injected market invariants
   (FND-002), then delivery/operations (FND-006, now partly fixed). The current
   ranking and evidence are in
   [`checkpoint-C/07-current-state.md`](../checkpoint-C/07-current-state.md).
2. **What behaviour was tested, and what happened?** The project tested the
   double-spend path, terminal resolution, the cooldown gate and the capacity
   burst, and observed them failing under deliberate weakening. The quality story
   is in [`checkpoint-C/02-quality-story.md`](../checkpoint-C/02-quality-story.md).
3. **What did review change, or deliberately keep?** Review changed the lock order
   in `close_trade` and added the cooldown-exit evidence while keeping the code.
   The review decisions are in
   [`checkpoint-C/03-review-decision.md`](../checkpoint-C/03-review-decision.md).
4. **What remains uncertain, and what happens next?** The residual risk register
   is [`checkpoint-C/05-residual-risk.md`](../checkpoint-C/05-residual-risk.md).
   FND-003 is fixed; FND-004, FND-006 and FND-002 are partly fixed; FND-007 and
   FND-008 remain open. FND-002 is partly fixed because the matrix covers 10
   observed-red mutations and the M-INV2 forced-failure rollback, but M-INV2
   cannot be observed-red without a second connection.

The strongest quality evidence is not a coverage number. It is the combination of
a business-rule oracle, a real database, committed-state assertions and observed
failure under fault injection.

## 8. Checkpoint E — delivery, observation and recovery

Checkpoint E is also a pre-read. The project now has a real local/CI delivery
baseline:

- `GET /api/health` returns app/database status without sensitive data.
- JSON logs record health failures and key action failures.
- `bun run smoke` checks health, the login UI and the home shell.
- the CI `Delivery and recovery` job starts Supabase, applies migrations, builds,
  starts the app, checks health, runs smoke, and exercises a backup/restore drill.
- the operations runbook covers release, rollback, backup/restore and incident
  steps.
- proposed SLOs and a p95 measurement method are recorded.
- the capacity burst records p50/p95/max; observed p95 is 463.11 ms and 571.68 ms.
- the recovery drill restored a 67,009-byte public-schema backup into a scratch
  database: tables 10→10, RLS policies 10→10, tournaments 2→2, marker present.

The evidence is in
[`checkpoint-E/README.md`](../checkpoint-E/README.md) and the
[delivery verification record](../verification/2026-11-111-delivery-operations-baseline.md)
and [recovery verification record](../verification/2026-11-117-recovery-drill.md).

The limits are explicit: no hosted deployment, external monitoring/alerting or
production recovery; the backup covers the application-owned `public` schema, not
Supabase-managed auth/storage data; p95 is an observation, not a hard gate.

## 9. Architecture and changeability in practice

The architecture is a modular monolith: one Next.js application, one PostgreSQL
database, domain modules in the schema/RPC surface, and a layered delivery shell.
The critical rules are enforced in the database because the domain requires
immediate, unambiguous answers under concurrent requests.

The idempotency change is the best current example of changeability:

- **Compatibility:** the request ID is optional in the RPC signature, so old
  callers can still call the function; the application always sends a key.
- **Data:** the new table is additive; no destructive migration.
- **Tests:** six new M-INV9 tests plus authorization coverage for
  `trade_requests`.
- **Guard:** the architecture guard scans the latest migration definitions and
  exact RPC signatures.
- **Release:** `v1.2.0`, with migration notes and known gaps.

The change did not alter pricing, settlement or authorization rules. It closed
FND-003 and left the legacy null-key path documented as non-idempotent.

## 11. Review, AI use and accountability

The project is one developer plus agent role passes. AI contributed
implementation, tests, documentation, review passes and release preparation. The
human project owner approved the plans, domain rules, architecture decisions,
releases and residual risk. The
[AI transparency log](../ai-transparency-log.md) records each contribution,
verification and limitation.

AI-generated code and tests can share assumptions, so the project does not treat a
green AI-generated suite as proof. The business-rule oracle, the observed-red
fault injections and the human decisions are the checks. The project also records
what AI got wrong and what was corrected.

The remaining review gap is FND-008: there is no independent human review. The
review evidence is structured self-review plus automated checks and agent role
passes. A peer review of one PR would close that gap.

### 11.1 OpenSpec adoption (2026-11-08)

OpenSpec was installed as a standalone CLI (`openspec 1.16.1`) and initialized
in the repository. The first bounded trial is the change
[`add-market-fault-injections`](../../../openspec/changes/archive/2026-11-08-add-market-fault-injections/proposal.md),
which targets the remaining fault-injection gap (FND-002).

- The change has a proposal, a design with a mutation catalog, and a tracked task
  list. It sets `skip_specs: true` because it adds verification evidence, not
  system behavior.
- `openspec validate add-market-fault-injections --type change --strict` passes.
- The change was implemented and verified in CI run
  [42474762811](https://github.com/match-pulse/match-pulse/actions/runs/42474762811):
  10 observed-red mutations pass, the M-INV2 forced-failure rollback test passes,
  and the full invariant suite returns to 28 pass / 0 fail.
- M-INV2 is not observed-red; PostgreSQL function atomicity cannot be broken
  without a second connection. FND-002 remains partly fixed for that reason.
- OpenSpec is a change-planning layer, not a second source of truth: the kata,
  domain model, invariant registry, ADRs, tests and verification records remain
  authoritative.
- The change is ready to archive after the documentation and trial review.

The adoption decision and its limits are recorded in
[`../development-practices.md`](../development-practices.md) §4.6 and
[`../../openspec/README.md`](../../../openspec/README.md). The evidence is in the

## 13. Lessons

- A green test is not evidence unless it can fail. Fault injection changed the
  project's claims more than any passing run.
- The documentation can assert a control that does not exist. CI originally ran
  lint and build but no domain test; the record was corrected.
- A retry is not a new business action. Request-key idempotency closed a gap the
  cooldown could not cover.
- A health endpoint must not depend on a user session; the first implementation
  logged a false database failure during prerender and was corrected.
- A backup is not a recovery until it has been restored and verified.
- The course vocabulary matters: calling a concurrency rule "integrity" is
  accurate in the domain but harder to map to the course's characteristics than
  "reliability".
